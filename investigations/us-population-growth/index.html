What we asked

How has the pace of United States population growth changed over the period covered by the World Bank’s annual population series?

This is intentionally a small example. Its job is to demonstrate the publication structure: a reader can inspect the preserved API response, run the transformation script, download the resulting table, and see the analytical code used in the article.

What the data shows

The U.S. population increased over the full series, but the annual rate of growth did not move in a straight line. The chart below lets readers inspect the level and year-over-year change separately.

The purpose of this demonstration is not to assign a cause. Population change reflects births, deaths, and migration, and this one series does not separate them. A responsible causal investigation would add those components and examine revisions and uncertainty in the underlying estimates.

Methodology

The retrieval script calls the World Bank Indicators API for SP.POP.TOTL and the country code USA. The raw JSON response is preserved byte-for-byte. The transformation script:

  1. verifies the expected response structure and indicator identifier;
  2. removes observations with no value;
  3. converts year and population to integers;
  4. sorts observations in ascending year order; and
  5. calculates year-over-year percentage change as (current / previous - 1) × 100.

No observations are manually added, removed, or edited. The processed CSV can be regenerated without network access:

python3 investigations/us-population-growth/scripts/analyze.py

To deliberately fetch a new source snapshot and then rebuild the analysis:

python3 investigations/us-population-growth/scripts/fetch_data.py
python3 investigations/us-population-growth/scripts/analyze.py

Limitations

  • This is a descriptive series, not a causal model.
  • The World Bank may revise historical observations; the committed snapshot is therefore part of the evidence.
  • The series is annual and cannot describe within-year changes.
  • A national total can conceal substantial differences between places and groups.
  • Growth calculated from rounded published values may differ slightly from a publisher’s separately calculated rate.

Sources

World Bank, Population, total — United States, indicator SP.POP.TOTL, retrieved through the World Bank Indicators API. Series definitions and metadata are available on the World Bank indicator page.
